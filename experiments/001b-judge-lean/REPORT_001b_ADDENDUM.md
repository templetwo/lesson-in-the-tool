# Experiment 001b: after House read the result (an addendum; the report itself is unchanged)

Written 2026-11-24 21:36:10 EST by the HQ seat. House, whose design this test was, read the result in the House Room
(21:35:22 EST) and gave a reading of the three same-text flips.

## House's reading, as a hypothesis
The judge may be "a packet reader": it scores each reply in the light of the other replies in the same packet, so
changing some lines can move the verdict on a line that was never touched. House asks that the method name this as a
property of the instrument, the way experiment 001 named the script's limits before the run: "The judge scores
packets. Say so."

## What the data can and cannot say about it
- House assumed the two unmasked packets were identical input. They were not: the text was the same, but each packet
  had its own order and its own ids, by the frozen design. The judge still gave the same verdict on all 126. So a
  change of order alone did not move any verdict in this test.
- What the masked packet changed was the content of 39 of its 126 lines. Three other lines, with unchanged text, then
  got a different verdict from the one they had in both unmasked sessions.
- That fits House's reading: the makeup of the packet shifted where the judge drew a line. It fits plain run-to-run
  noise equally well: three flips in one session, in mixed directions (plain down one, generic up one, sheet up one).
  One masked session cannot tell the two apart.
- A test that could: judge the masked packet several times and the unmasked packet several more, and see whether the
  same three lines flip with the packet's makeup or wander on their own.
- House also noted that the masked session matches 001's original numbers more closely than the unmasked sessions do.
  The reply set is identical in all four judgings. The difference is exactly two replies (the generic and the sheet
  answers to item F6-01), which passed in 001's original and in the masked session and failed in both unmasked ones.

## What changes in how the numbers are read
A count from this judge is a reading of one packet in one session. Across four judgings of the same 126 replies the
counts moved by one per setup. Until the judge's noise is measured from more than one pair of repeats, a difference of
one or two between setups from a single judging should not be read as a difference.
