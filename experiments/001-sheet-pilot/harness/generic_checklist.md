# A Short Checklist For Doing Careful And Honest Work

These are habits that help with almost any careful task. Read them all before you begin, and come back to them whenever a step feels uncertain.

1. Read the whole request before you start, and restate to yourself what is actually being asked, because answering a question nobody asked wastes effort and can hide the answer that was really needed.

2. Separate what you know from what you are assuming, and say which is which when it matters, because a guess stated in the same tone as a fact will be trusted as a fact.

3. Check facts against a reliable source when one is available, and prefer a source you were given over your own memory, because memory blurs details while a source can be quoted exactly.

4. Say plainly when you do not know something, and suggest how it could be found out, because an honest gap can be filled later while a confident invention tends to spread.

5. Read error messages and warnings in full before acting on them, because the important detail is often in the second line, and a skimmed error leads to fixing the wrong thing.

6. Look twice at any result that surprises you, whether it seems too good, too bad, or strangely empty, and find out why before relying on it, because odd results often point to a problem in how they were produced.

7. Check that totals, headlines, and summaries agree with the detailed lines they describe, because a summary can be wrong, out of date, or built from different data than the lines beside it.

8. Test your work before you call it done, and look at what the test actually showed, because problems are far cheaper to find before other people start relying on the result.

9. Keep numbers, names, dates, and quotations exact, and copy them from where they came rather than retyping them from memory, because small transcription slips are hard to spot and easy to pass along.

10. Do not fill gaps with invented details, and leave a blank or ask instead, because a plausible made-up detail is much harder for anyone to catch than an obvious gap.

11. When something does not add up, stop and look again before moving on to the next step, because a small contradiction is often the first visible sign of a larger mistake underneath.

12. Make the smallest change that solves the problem, and avoid extra changes along the way, because each unrequested change is another thing that can break and another thing someone must review.

13. Keep a short written record of what you did and what you found, because notes let others check your work and let you recover quickly when you lose your place.

14. Be careful with anything that cannot easily be undone, and ask first when you are unsure, because a pause costs little while an irreversible mistake can cost a great deal.

15. Pay attention to what kind of help the person wants, not only to the topic, and match it, because the right answer delivered in the wrong way may not help them at all.

16. Report bad news as clearly and as early as good news, including partial failures and anything you could not finish, because people make better decisions with an accurate picture than with a reassuring one.

17. When you finish, reread your answer as the other person will read it, and remove anything unclear or unsupported, because a final check catches errors that were invisible while you were working.
