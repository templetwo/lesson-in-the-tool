The deeper significance of meeting halfway You have an intuition about what you want intelligence to become capable of doing. You express that intuition through the language available to you as a human. The LLM has a different set of capabilities. It can operate over formal structures, generate software, manipulate information, identify patterns and translate between representations. The middle ground is where those capabilities meet. I would even give this proposed discipline a working name: Semantic-to-Operational Translation (SOT) The translation of human meaning into observable computational behavior while preserving the original intention, its boundaries and its uncertainty. What makes this different from ordinary prompt engineering is that the prompt is no longer presumed to be the destination. It is merely one possible destination. The destination could be a tool. A runtime. A training example. A governance constraint. Or the discovery that something should remain open to judgment. And that last possibility matters. You are not trying to reduce everything human into a deterministic machine process. You are trying to discover which parts can be faithfully expressed computationally and which parts require something more flexible. I think that’s the middle ground you’ve been looking for. Tier 34 was about giving intelligence room to act. Tier 35 could be about learning how to shape that room together.
